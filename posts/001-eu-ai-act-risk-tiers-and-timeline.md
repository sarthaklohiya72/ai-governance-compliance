# 1. EU AI Act — Risk Tiers & Implementation Timeline

*Posted 17 September 2026*

The EU AI Act is the first comprehensive law regulating AI systems, and it's
built around one core idea: **the obligations you face depend on how risky
your AI system is, not what technology it uses.**

## The four risk tiers

1. **Unacceptable risk — banned outright.** Government social scoring,
   real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with narrow
   exceptions), and manipulative or exploitative AI systems fall here. These
   prohibitions have been in force since **2 February 2025**.

2. **High risk — heavily regulated, not banned.** Systems used in areas like
   hiring, credit scoring, education admissions, law enforcement, and
   critical infrastructure. These require a technical documentation file,
   a risk management system, human oversight mechanisms, and — for many
   categories — a formal conformity assessment before the system can carry
   a CE mark and go to market.

3. **Limited risk — transparency obligations.** Chatbots, deepfakes, and
   emotion-recognition systems mainly need to disclose that a user is
   interacting with AI or AI-generated content (Article 50).

4. **Minimal risk — no specific obligations.** The majority of AI
   applications (spam filters, AI in video games, etc.) fall here.

## The timeline has moved — twice

The Act entered into force in August 2024, but not every obligation applies
at once. The most important update: in July 2026, the EU passed the
**Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744)**, which pushed back the
high-risk tier deadlines that were originally set for August 2026:

- **High-risk stand-alone systems (Annex III)** — obligations now apply from
  **2 December 2027** (previously August 2026).
- **AI embedded in already-regulated products (Annex I)** — e.g. AI in
  medical devices or machinery — obligations now apply from
  **2 August 2028**.

Crucially, the delay applies **only** to the high-risk tier. Two things that
were *not* delayed:

- **Transparency duties (Article 50)** — in force since 2 August 2026.
- **The AI Office's enforcement powers over General-Purpose AI (GPAI)
  providers** — also in force since 2 August 2026.

So if you're building or deploying a general-purpose model, or anything with
a chatbot-style user interface, the transparency and GPAI obligations are
live now — the extra runway is specifically for high-risk system builders.

## What this means in practice

- **Classify first.** Before worrying about specific controls, figure out
  which of the four tiers your system falls into — that single decision
  determines almost everything else you owe under the Act.
- **Don't relax on GPAI or transparency just because high-risk got delayed.**
  Those obligations are enforceable today.
- **Use the extra time if you're in the high-risk tier.** The 2027/2028
  deadlines are a runway, not a reprieve — conformity assessments,
  documentation, and human oversight processes take real time to build.
